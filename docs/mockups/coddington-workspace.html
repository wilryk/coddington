Coddington Workspace

Seven screens of the proposed 3D-first restructure, drawn in the app's own visual language. The idea in one line: the 3D scene is the workspace, not a results viewer — geometry and corner rays are live from the moment the app opens, and every parameter is editable from a sidebar stage and by clicking the object in the scene, always in sync.

Rev 5 changes from your fourth review: optical-error defaults corrected to the manuscript (90 % reflectance, no slope or specularity error); the receiver is now a real design — type per layout (flat everywhere; cylindrical and frustum under prime focus) plus the paper's entrance aperture with an offset receiver behind it (screen 4's sidebar); degenerate geometries (axicon half angle 0°, Cassegrain R → ∞) are legal and trace as flat mirrors with an info badge; a Year estimate joins Analysis (sample days across the year, DNI-weighted interpolation, MWh/yr); and branding is placed — a quiet Ryker Optics lockup at the bottom of the sidebar with an About link, and a dashed placeholder where the Coddington logo will go.

The companion spec is docs/ui-spec.md in the repo. Nothing gets built until you approve both.

1The Workspace — live field, no trace needed

Left: the four design stages in workflow order — Heliostat → Field → Receiver & Tower → Sun. Center: the always-live 3D viewport with auto-refreshing corner rays. Bottom: fidelity, Run, and the latest results with a trace timestamp.

Rev 5: the dashed square in the top bar is the placeholder for the Coddington logo; the Ryker Optics lockup sits quietly at the bottom of the sidebar with the About link.

2Click the cone — one source of truth, with warnings

Selecting the axicon secondary opens a floating inspector showing exactly the same fields as the sidebar stage, always in sync — no hidden overrides, no separate Apply. Here the half angle was raised to 22.5°: the solution is still legal, so the aperture radius shows an amber warning — "needs ≥ 15 800 mm" — with dashed red rays where the outer field misses the cone. Rev 3 adds your near-field case: a heliostat so close its aim point can't reach the cone at all is highlighted red, its miss ray dashed, and the warning counts it. Nothing is grown automatically. An impossible geometry shows red at the field instead, and the scene keeps the last valid shape.

Rev 3: near-heliostat total-miss flagged (red mirror + counted in the warning). Same treatment applies whatever caused it — half angle, apex height, or a heliostat dragged too close in plan view.

3Field plan view — opens with the Field stage

Expanding the Field stage switches the viewport to a top-down plan: rings at the layout radii, the receiver and axicon aperture at center, every heliostat visible relative to them. Drag a heliostat to move it (an override, counted with a reset), double-click empty ground to add one. Rev 4: the layout picker now includes honeycomb (Fermat spiral default; radial staggered, grid, honeycomb, imported CSV), and roads take any form: here an 8 m access road on the South half only — preserving the high-performance North field — plus a 6 m ring road at 60 m, together cutting 62 heliostats.

Rev 4: roads support arbitrary angle, partial extent (e.g. South-only), and rings; the sidebar counts what they cut.

4Tower elevation — opens with the Receiver & Tower stage

Expanding Receiver & Tower switches to a to-scale side elevation with SolidWorks-style dimensions. Rev 3: all heights reference the heliostat plane — the dash-dot datum line through the pivots — not the ground, and a ground offset (heliostat plane above ground) is its own dimension and sidebar field. Apex height, receiver height, aperture radius, and half angle are editable value boxes tied to the same fields as the sidebar.

Rev 5: the sidebar gains a Receiver section — type (flat window here; cylindrical and frustum offered under prime focus), window size, and the paper's aperture → receiver offset (0 = receiver at the entrance aperture, today's behavior). Also encoded: half angle 0° or an infinite-radius Cassegrain are legal and trace as flat mirrors with an info badge, never an error.

5The Library — swap receivers, keep the field

Three collections: heliostat designs, receiver configs, projects. Built-ins are the manuscript's numbers, locked — duplicate one to tweak it. "Use" swaps the receiver config while the field and heliostat design stay put: the compare-receivers workflow. New in rev 2: Import / Export for SolTrace and SolarPILOT (plus plain positions CSV) — a project can round-trip for cross-checking in either tool. Old saved setups arrive under Projects as imports.

Rev 3: the drawer footer now shows the storage folder with a Change button — pick any working/project directory (a shared drive, a repo); the default is a Coddington folder in your home directory. SolTrace/SolarPILOT round-trips include sun shape and optical-error settings where a direct equivalent exists, with anything unmapped reported.

6Heliostat Shape — the full-screen detail editor

Replaces the viewport while open. Custom outline sketching, explicit canting (locked under Twisting), jet sag map with stated contour interval. Rev 4 adds two things: an Optical errors section (slope error, specularity, reflectance — used by Monte Carlo and carried through SolTrace/SolarPILOT export), and an answer to "which heliostat am I looking at": the header chip and sag panel name the previewed heliostat (H-214, r 45.2 m), a locator mini-map shows where it sits in the field, the ▾ selector switches, and clicking any heliostat in the workspace offers "View shape" to open this tab on it.

Rev 5: error defaults are now the manuscript's — 90 % reflectance, zero slope and specularity error.

7Analysis — day sweeps against the current project

The strip under the tabs restates exactly what is being swept. Day sweeps run in the background with progress and cancel; results are the energy-through-the-day plot, the timestep table, and CSV export. Rev 2 answers "how do I see irradiance maps": click any timestep row and its flux map appears beside the table; the workspace's flux thumbnail expands to the same view.

Rev 5: the Year estimate strip — trace a set of sample days spread across the year (default 12, spaced in solar declination), interpolate between them weighted by DNI, and report annual collection in MWh. Saved with the project like day sweeps. Check: is 12 declination-spaced days the right default sampling?

Decisions these mockups encode

  • Geometry is always live — the scene renders on open and after every edit; a trace is only ever for physics.
  • The viewport follows the stage — Field opens the plan view, Receiver & Tower opens the dimensioned elevation, everything else is 3D; "back to 3D" is always one click.
  • Corner rays auto-refresh on edits (capped to a spread-out subset on big fields); full traces stay manual.
  • Warnings vs. errors — a legal-but-underfilled geometry warns in amber with the needed value and never auto-adjusts; heliostats that cannot hit the optics at all are highlighted red and counted; an impossible geometry errors in red and the scene holds the last valid shape.
  • Apply only where it's slow — edits apply live when the refresh is fast; any operation expected to take more than a couple of seconds (huge layouts, expensive re-solves) shows an explicit Apply instead.
  • Panel + in-scene editing are the same thing — same fields, same values, messages inline in both.
  • Honest labels per layout — "Apex height", "Focus height", "Secondary vertex height"; the ambiguous shared "tower height" box is gone.
  • Field layouts — Fermat spiral (default), radial staggered, grid, honeycomb, imported CSV; roads of any angle, extent, or ring form cut out the heliostats they cross.
  • Heights reference the heliostat plane (confirmed: the engine's z = 0), with ground offset (default 2 500 mm) as its own parameter.
  • Optical errors live in Heliostat Shape — slope error, specularity, reflectance (manuscript defaults: 0 / 0 / 90 %) — and the previewed heliostat is always named, locatable, and switchable.
  • The receiver is a design — flat window everywhere, cylindrical and frustum under prime focus, and an entrance aperture with the receiver offset behind it by a chosen distance.
  • Degenerate limits are legal — half angle 0° or R → ∞ trace as flat mirrors with an info badge instead of erroring.
  • Annual energy — a Year estimate traces sample days across the year and interpolates to MWh/yr.
  • Branding stays quiet — Ryker Optics lockup + About at the bottom of the sidebar; app-logo placeholder in the top bar until the real mark exists.
  • Named library with locked manuscript built-ins in a user-chosen storage folder; projects replace saved setups (with import); SolTrace / SolarPILOT / CSV interchange including sun shape and optical errors where equivalents exist.
  • Analysis runs save with the project, with per-run discard and a storage manager.
  • Custom heliostat outlines — polygon sketch with typed dimensions replaces the flower type; canting is explicit (Off / Auto / Focal) and locked under Twisting.
  • Viewing scales to 10,000 heliostats; tracing keeps its current 1,000 cap and says so.
